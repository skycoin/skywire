## The ≤ 45 MB check (item 1.7), and what G8 should compare

**The check is not met in-app, and the explanation is accepted.** As written
("the 1.4 gauge settles at ≤ 45 MB idle") the criterion has two readings.
Idle-with-the-core-never-started *is* ≤ 45 (34.8 MiB here, 35 MB author) —
but that reading is vacuous, since the extension will always have the core
running. The reading that matters, the one G0's 42–45 MB host number came
from (the core alone as the process, limit on, no traffic), fails in-app:
59–66 MB for the author, a 54.6–69.9 MiB minute-band here. The 45 MB was
measured on a process that was nothing but the core; the app's process also
carries SwiftUI, the app frameworks and the Simulator's runtime state —
~25–33 MB before and beside the core, which the packet-tunnel extension will
not carry. The evidence README splits this honestly: Go's share (untagged
VM_ALLOCATE + the core image's dirty `__DATA`), the part that actually moves
into the extension, stayed at 26–31 MB — under the 40 MiB cap in both runs,
with the limit not binding at idle traffic and no upward drift over the soak
(this review's minute samples fall as often as they rise; `footprint -p`
shows 20 MB reclaimable at 10 min, i.e. Go returning pages). The in-app
gauge is the wrong estimator for the extension budget; Go's share is the
right one, and it is comfortably inside it.

**Proposed G8 criterion ("within 10 % of G1"): compare Go's share, not the
whole-app gauge.** Concretely: at 10 min connected with the 40 MiB limit,
`footprint -p`'s untagged VM_ALLOCATE (dirty) plus the core image's dirty
`__DATA` must stay within 10 % of the top of G1's measured band — i.e.
**≤ 33 MB** (G1: 26–31 MB) — and `GOMEMLIMIT set to 40MiB` must still log at
start. The whole-gauge table should still be recorded for continuity, but a
10 % band applied to the app gauge would both false-alarm on framework noise
and mask a real core regression beneath it. The ≤ 45 MB question itself
moves to Lane D3, pointed at what it was always about: the extension's
`phys_footprint` against the ~50 MB jetsam cap on a device.

- Proven here, in-app on the Simulator: the Go core builds for both iOS
  slices and runs inside an iOS app process; lifecycle across the C boundary
  under the documented ownership rules (start/stop/restart, stop-during-start
  within budget, failure reasons); the local API reachable from the Mac's
  loopback; suspension freezes the in-app core while its listening socket
  survives (no reclaim on the Simulator); force-quit tears the listener down;
  remembered-Connect restores the core after a kill; the 40 MiB GOMEMLIMIT
  applies; footprint magnitudes and their category split.
- Remains for Lane D: the packet-tunnel extension as the real host (D1–D3:
  bring-up, loopback vs provider messages, the ~50 MB jetsam cap on a
  device); device socket-reclaim for a suspended `InAppCoreHost` (D1 — the
  extension is not suspended by design); traffic-driven memory (VPN, SOCKS,
  chat load, M3/M4); device start times (the 18–31 s Startup completes here
  are Simulator + production-network discovery, not iOS).

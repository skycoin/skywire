    dmsg-async: store and forward for offline keys¶

    Status: proposed. Nothing here is implemented yet. Once it is, the behaviour moves into a skywire-specs document and this note records the reasoning.

    Problem¶

    A dmsg stream needs both ends online at once. A client that wants to reach an offline key has to keep trying on its own, and that fails when the sender goes offline too: a browser tab that closes, a phone that sleeps, a laptop that is shut.

    Skymail covers part of this with its Outbox and helpers. The sender's own visors carry queued mail and retry. That still needs some visor on the sender's side to be online when the recipient is. Skychat 1:1 messages, file offers and group join requests have nothing at all.

    RFC #3005 asked for store and forward in dmsg. #5946 implemented it on the dmsg servers and was closed, for three reasons.

    1. Every message sat in the memory of shared deployment servers, even with a storage directory set.
    2. Anyone could deposit for any key. That got around the recipient's whitelist, because the server took the message whether or not the recipient would.
    3. The servers were picked by hashing the recipient's key over every server that offered the feature. The recipient never chose who held its mail.

    This design keeps the parts of #5946 that worked (the framed protocol, the fetch and ack model, the limits and the client API) and changes who holds the messages and who decides what they accept.

    Model¶

                     deposit(R, port, sealed payload)
   sender S ───────────────────────────────────────▶ inbox host H
      │                                                  ▲   │
      │ look up R's inbox record                         │   │ fetch / ack
      ▼                                         register │   ▼
   contacts cache, then dmsg discovery ◀──── post ── recipient R
    • Recipient R chooses one or more inbox hosts. It signs an inbox record naming them, registers with each host, and fetches from them when it comes online.
    • Inbox host H is an ordinary dmsg client that serves the async protocol on dmsg port 85. It is normally a visor R owns that stays online, such as its hypervisor or a home server. It holds messages only for keys that registered with it, and only from senders their policy allows.
    • Sender S finds R's inbox record, seals the payload for R and deposits it with R's hosts when R itself cannot be reached.

    The dmsg servers only relay the streams, as they do for every other port. They store nothing. Store and forward becomes a property of the dmsg address space, but the storage lives on hardware the recipient controls.

    What it is not¶

    • Not a mailbox. Payloads are small (64 KiB by default) and short-lived (7 days by default). Large mail stays with the sender and its helpers.
    • Not reliable delivery. A host may be down, full or slow. The sender keeps its copy and its own retries. dmsg-async is one more path.
    • Not anonymous. The host sees who sends to whom. It does not see sealed payloads.
    • Not open on the deployment. A deployment may run a host under a service key for users who have no second visor. It uses the disk store and holds only for keys that registered with it, exactly as any other host does.

    Inbox record¶

    The recipient signs the record with its visor key. It says where to deposit, not who may deposit, so it reveals R's hosts but not R's contacts.

    Field Type Meaning
    pk hex key the recipient
    hosts array of hex keys the inbox hosts, in the order senders try them, at most 4
    max_message integer the largest payload every host accepts, in bytes
    seq integer increases on every change, so an older record never replaces a newer one
    expires RFC 3339 time at most 30 days after signing
    sig hex signature over the canonical JSON of the other fields

    R re-signs the record when its hosts change and before it expires. A record with an empty hosts list withdraws dmsg-async for R.

    Where senders find it¶

    1. Contacts. A sender keeps the newest record it has seen for each contact. A sender R allows is usually one R talks to, so this covers most deposits without a lookup.
    2. dmsg discovery. R posts the record to a new endpoint, POST /dmsg-discovery/inbox/{pk}, and senders read it with GET /dmsg-discovery/inbox/{pk}. Discovery checks the signature and the sequence and keeps the record until expires. This is separate from the client entry because a client entry expires 60 minutes after its last registration, which is exactly when R is offline. A separate endpoint also leaves the entry format and its signature check untouched.
    3. The host. A sender that already knows one host can ask it for R's current record.

    A recipient MAY skip step 2 to keep its hosts private. Only senders that already have its record can then deposit.

    Registration and policy¶

    R registers with each host over the async protocol. The registration carries the signed inbox record and R's sender policy, which only the host sees.

    Policy field Meaning
    allow sender keys that may deposit. Empty means any key
    deny sender keys that may never deposit
    max_per_sender messages one sender may have waiting, default 20

    The host accepts a registration only when both hold.

    1. The host agrees to hold for R. R is the host's own key, a visor it manages as a hypervisor, or a key in its hosted list. A host never holds for a key it was not configured to accept.
    2. R names the host. The record is valid, unexpired, signed by R and lists the host.

    Skymail sets allow from its own whitelist, so a key that the mailbox would refuse over SMTP cannot park mail for it either. Other consumers that need a narrower policy than skymail's use their own port filter on fetch.

    Protocol¶

    The host serves dmsg port 85 (skyenv.DmsgAsyncPort). Every dmsg stream runs a Noise KK handshake between the two client keys (Stream.prepareFields in pkg/dmsg/dmsg/stream.go), so the host knows which key opened the stream whatever server or relay carried it, and the request needs no signature of its own.

    The stream carries frames: a 4-byte big-endian length and then the bytes. A control frame is JSON, at most 64 KiB. A payload frame is raw bytes, at most max_message. One operation runs per stream, and the stream closes after the reply.

    Operation Who Request Reply
    register R {"op":"register","record":{...},"policy":{...}} {"ok":true} or an error
    unregister R {"op":"unregister"} {"ok":true,"dropped":n}
    record anyone {"op":"record","pk":<hex>} {"ok":true,"record":{...}}
    deposit S {"op":"deposit","to":<hex>,"port":n,"size":n,"ref":<hex>}, then the payload frame {"ok":true,"id":<hex>} or an error
    fetch R {"op":"fetch","ports":[n,...]} {"ok":true,"messages":[header,...],"more":bool}, then one payload frame per header
    ack R {"op":"ack","ids":[<hex>,...]} {"ok":true,"deleted":n}
    • The sender comes from the session. A deposit records the stream's remote key as the sender. The request cannot name another.
    • ref makes a deposit idempotent. It is 16 bytes the sender picks per message. A second deposit with the same sender, recipient and ref returns the first id and stores nothing, so a sender that retries does not fill the recipient's quota with copies.
    • Fetch and ack are for the asking key only. R fetches its own messages on the ports it lists, oldest first, up to 32 messages or 1 MiB per reply.
    • Nothing is deleted without an ack. R acks only what its handlers took. A handler error leaves the message for the next fetch or until it expires.

    A header is {"id","from","port","size","deposited"}. Errors come back as {"ok":false,"code":...} with one of not_hosted, not_allowed, too_big, recipient_full, sender_full, store_full, bad_record, bad_request, internal.

    Host storage and limits¶

    The host has two stores, chosen by store.

    • disk, the default. Each message is a file <dir>/<recipient hex>/<id>, written whole and renamed into place. The host keeps only the headers in memory and reads a payload from disk when a fetch sends it. At start it drops expired, half-written and unreadable files and rebuilds its counts. A deployment host and any host where memory is tight use this store.
    • memory. Payloads are held in memory and a restart loses them. It suits a host with memory to spare and no disk to write to, and tests. max_total_size bounds it the same way.

    Expired messages are removed every 10 minutes in both.

    Limit Setting Default
    Largest payload max_message 64 KiB, at most 1 MiB
    Messages per recipient max_per_recipient 200
    Bytes per recipient max_recipient_bytes 8 MiB
    Messages per sender per recipient the recipient's max_per_sender 20
    Whole store max_total_size 256 MiB
    Time to live ttl 7 days

    A zero value takes the default. The host's limits bound what a registration may ask for. A recipient's max_message above the host's own is refused.

    Recipient behaviour¶

    • R fetches from each host at start, whenever it opens a new dmsg session to a host, every 10 minutes while online, and when a consumer asks.
    • R re-registers with each host whenever its record or policy changes and at least once a day, so a host that lost its state recovers.
    • A host is usually the recipient's own visor, so it is often online when R is. The fetch on each new session is what delivers mail after R comes back.

    Sender behaviour¶

    A consumer calls Send(ctx, R, port, payload).

    1. Try a direct dmsg stream to R on the port, as today.
    2. If R cannot be reached, find R's inbox record. With none, the send fails as it does today and the consumer's own retries apply.
    3. Seal the payload for R unless the consumer sealed it already.
    4. Deposit on R's hosts in order until one accepts. Depositing on a second host is optional and costs a duplicate, which the recipient's consumer drops.

    Sealing uses the skymail envelope (skymail.Seal in pkg/skymail/sealed.go, or a Sealer in an app that has no secret key): the payload is encrypted to R's key and signed by S. The recipient's consumer checks the signature, so it never has to trust the host's from header.

    Consumers¶

    Consumer Port Use
    Skymail 25 the Outbox deposits queued mail for R once it is past the first retry, and R files fetched mail through the same delivery checks as SMTP
    Skychat 1:1 1 a message to an offline contact is deposited, and fetched messages enter history as if received live
    Group join requests and admissions 8060 a request waits for an offline admin, and the admission, which carries the group key, waits for a joiner who went offline
    Contact and pairing requests 1 a request waits for the other visor instead of failing
    Missed call and file offer notices 62, 64 a short notice waits. The call or the file itself still needs both ends online
    Hypervisor to managed visor 46 a config change or update request waits for a managed visor that is offline

    Every consumer sends small messages that only need to arrive eventually. Large or live traffic (file contents, voice, VPN, proxy) stays on direct streams.

    The first implementation is the library, the host, and skymail and skychat 1:1 as its first consumers. The others follow.

    Configuration¶

    In the visor config, with live changes saved in dmsg-async/settings.json beside the local path so a wasm visor keeps them across a reload.

    "dmsg_async": {
  "host": {
    "enable": true,
    "store": "disk",
    "dir": "dmsg-async",
    "hosted": [],
    "max_message": 65536,
    "max_total_size": 268435456,
    "ttl": "168h"
  },
  "inbox": {
    "hosts": [],
    "publish": true
  }
}
    • host.enable is off by default except on a visor that manages other visors as a hypervisor, where it is on and hosts those visors. That is the case skymail already covers with carrying, and the wasm tab's hypervisor is the natural inbox for the tab.
    • inbox.hosts defaults to this visor's hypervisors when it has any, and is otherwise empty, which turns dmsg-async off for this key.
    • inbox.publish posts the record to dmsg discovery.

    The CLI gets skywire cli async with status, inbox, hosts, register and fetch, and the same settings as skywire cli mail settings.

    Compatibility¶

    • No change to dmsg sessions, stream requests or discovery entries. The protocol runs on a new port over ordinary streams, so older servers relay it and older clients never see it.
    • Older dmsg discovery returns 404 for the inbox endpoint. Senders then rely on contacts and hosts, and recipients post again later.
    • Older visors do not serve port 85. A recipient lists only hosts that answered its register.

    Security considerations¶

    • Whitelist. The host applies the recipient's sender policy to the session-authenticated sender key. A key the recipient refuses cannot deposit.
    • Who holds what. A host holds only for keys it was configured to accept and that named it in a signed record. It cannot be made to hold for an arbitrary key.
    • What a host sees. Sender key, recipient key, port, size and time. Not a sealed payload. A recipient that uses a host it does not own trusts that host with this metadata.
    • What discovery shows. A published record links R to its hosts, usually R's own other visors. A recipient that wants to hide this sets inbox.publish to false and gives its record to contacts directly.
    • Dishonest hosts. A host may drop, delay or replay messages. It cannot forge or read a sealed payload. Consumers drop repeats, skymail by sender and Message-ID.
    • Abuse. Bounded per sender, per recipient and in total, on each host. Only senders the recipient allows can use a recipient's share at all.
    • Stale records. seq stops an old record from replacing a newer one, and expires bounds how long a withdrawn host keeps receiving deposits.

    Implementation plan¶

    1. pkg/dmsg/dmsgasync: frames, records and their signatures, the disk store with its limits, the host handler, and the client (Register, Fetch, Deposit, Send, Handle(port, fn)). Tests with real dmsg servers and clients from pkg/dmsg/dmsgtest.
    2. dmsg discovery: the inbox record endpoint and store, with the record's signature and sequence checks.
    3. Visor: the host and the inbox client, configuration, settings file, CLI, and visor state fields.
    4. Skymail: deposit from the Outbox and filing of fetched mail.
    5. Skychat 1:1: deposit when the contact is offline, and fetched messages into history.
    6. A spec in skywire-specs, then the other consumers.

    Each step is tested against live visors before it merges: a recipient behind its hypervisor as host, the :8443 wasm tab as a recipient, and a sender that goes offline before the recipient comes back.

    Open questions¶

    • Which keys may register with a deployment host, and with what limits. The host itself uses the disk store.
    • Should a host also push to the recipient when it sees R come online, instead of waiting for R's fetch? Fetching on each new session covers the common case, so push is left out for now.
